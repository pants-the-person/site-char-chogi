Kugane is a city that smells of salt, tea, and money. I liked it immediately.

The first thing I did was wire a hefty sum of coin back to Ul'dah and place a back-alley bounty on the pirate gang that had raided my ship. The second thing I did was find a quiet inn where nobody asked why a Lalafell with horns was paying in Ul'dahn coin.

The debt was settled. I did not attend the funerals. I sent flowers.

With that finished, I got to work. Hingashi angling is a different discipline. Their rods are longer, their lines are finer, and their patience is the same. I learned to read a river the way I read a room. I learned which fish are worth catching and which are worth selling. I learned that a man who is very good at a harmless thing is rarely searched.

The rest of my time was quieter. My smuggling routes already ran east; I turned them toward the rebels resisting the Garlean Empire. Supplies. Intelligence. The occasional letter that needed to arrive without a seal. I did not do it for patriotism. I did it because the Empire is bad for business, and because a favor stored in the right pocket is worth more than coin.

The Empire has a long memory. So do I. Mine is better organized.

I did not stay to watch the bounty pay out. That is what trusted people are for. I went fishing.
