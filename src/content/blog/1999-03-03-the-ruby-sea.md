I wanted to expand east. The Ruby Sea had other plans.

I booked passage on a trade vessel bound for Kugane. The ship was respectable, the cargo was legitimate, and the captain was paid enough not to ask about the third thing. We were three days out when the pirates came over the rail.

I did the arithmetic. I am 2'11". A direct fight would have been a very short story, and I have never been fond of short stories.

So I sealed myself inside a reinforced salted-fish barrel and rolled overboard.

It was not dignified. It was effective.

The sea is patient in a way that makes a man either religious or quiet. I drifted. Eventually I washed ashore on a deserted island with a pocketknife, a barrel hoop, and enough shipwreck to build a rod. I fished. I ate raw catch and drank rainwater. I learned the difference between hunger and patience, which is mostly that one of them can be cured.

Three months. No ship. No sign. Just the tide, the rock, and a small man arguing with a fish.

When a merchant vessel finally picked me up, I had sharpened my patience, my resolve, and my opinion of pirates. I had also stopped being surprised by anything the sea did. That has been useful since.

There are people in Kugane who still tell the story of the man who arrived in a barrel. I have never confirmed it. I have also never denied it. A reputation is a tool. You do not throw away a good one just because it smells faintly of fish.

I do not recommend the cruise. The fishing was excellent.
