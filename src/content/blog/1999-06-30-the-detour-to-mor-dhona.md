I came back to Ul'dah a master angler with heavy pockets and a reputation that made people lower their voices. That is the version of me the city saw.

The other version was lonely. I did not use that word at the time.

After a wild night—the kind with too much rum and a winning hand at the wrong table—I picked up a brooding Xaela warrior to share my bed. He was tall enough to block out the sun. I am not. It was a good arrangement.

It became an ongoing one. He was a bodyguard with benefits, and I told myself it was practical. A man in my position needs someone who can reach the top shelf and the people on it. He did not care about my underworld title. He cared whether I had eaten. That was more disarming than any blade.

We set out on a detour-filled fishing trip toward Mor Dhona. There was gambling. There was drinking. There was the kind of camaraderie that sneaks up on a man who has spent his life keeping everyone at arm's length. For the first time in years, I was catching genuine feelings for someone who had no use for the Don.

I was going to say something. I had a whole speech prepared. It was very good.

Then an aetheric rift tore open near the crystal fields and pulled me into the dark.

We never cast a line. That is the part that still bothers me. Not the rift. The wasted trip.
