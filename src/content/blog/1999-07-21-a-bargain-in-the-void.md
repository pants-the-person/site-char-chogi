The Void is not a place. It is a pressure.

There is no weather. No horizon. No sound except the one your own heart makes when it realizes it is still beating. Time stopped meaning anything. I measured the hours in how often the dark aether surged through me and came out as light.

It was not a pleasant light. It was purple and black and violent. It cracked stone. It cracked me. I survived by staying small, staying quiet, and refusing to be interesting to the things that noticed me.

One of them noticed anyway.

It wore the face of a Lalafell researcher—round spectacles, ink-stained fingers, a smile that had been practiced in front of a mirror. It said it could teach me discipline. It said the aether bleeding out of my body was a resource. It said we could help each other.

I have made worse deals in Ul'dah. Not many.

It taught me **Black Magic**. In exchange, it fed on the excess aether my body could not hold. I learned to shape the dark instead of being shaped by it. I became formidable. I gained moderate control. The taint stayed. It is still there, under the skin, waiting for a bad night.

When I had learned enough, I mustered the full weight of it, tore a seam in the rift, and forced my way back into the mortal realm.

I came back with power. I also came back with a leak. The two are related.

If you see me watching my own hands, do not interrupt. I am doing arithmetic.
